Weather satellites like Meteor-M broadcast their pictures openly to Earth, and the ISS transmits on 145.8 MHz. Downlink tells you when they pass over you, and with a small RTL-SDR stick on your phone you receive them yourself. No internet, no account, no ads.

<b>With just your phone</b>
<ul>
<li>Passes of Meteor-M, ISS, Tiangong, Hubble and 14 more objects, rated "will it work?"</li>
<li>Sky dome with compass, camera AR view, 3D globe with day and night</li>
<li>Naked-eye visibility, reminders before good passes, home screen widget</li>
</ul>

<b>With an SDR stick and antenna</b>
<ul>
<li>Pair the stick over USB, live spectrum and waterfall</li>
<li>FM audio from the ISS and amateur radio satellites</li>
<li>Doppler tracking and automatic start at the pass</li>
<li>Meteor-M LRPT: recording and a built-in decoder, the colour picture goes to your photos</li>
<li>Calibration: frequency error against an FM station, best gain, interference check</li>
<li>Build guide for magnetic base and V-dipole, shopping guide for the accessories</li>
</ul>

You need an RTL-SDR stick (for example RTL-SDR Blog V3 or Nooelec NESDR SMArt), an antenna and an OTG adapter, about 55 to 60 euros together.

The app is in German for now. Orbits: SGP4 with CelesTrak elements. Receiver: librtlsdr and libusb. Open source under GPL-3.0.

Parts of the app are written with an AI assistant. I test every version on a real phone.
